Ethiopia's national dialogue has endorsed merging the prime minister and president roles, paving the way for a potential executive presidency for Abiy Ahmed. This proposal, aimed at an overhaul of the political system, faces opposition warnings about concentrating power, and still requires constitutional approval.

For investors, this signals potential for significant shifts in governance and decision-making. While a strong executive can fast-track projects and policy implementation, it also introduces risks associated with weakened checks and balances, potentially impacting regulatory stability, rule of law, and long-term political predictability. It could centralize authority but also elevate political risk.

Monitor the constitutional approval process closely and the specifics of the new executive powers. Pay attention to any public or political backlash, as well as the government's rhetoric regarding institutional safeguards. The actual implementation and its impact on institutional strength will define future investment sentiment.
