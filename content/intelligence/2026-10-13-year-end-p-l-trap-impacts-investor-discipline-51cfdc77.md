BusinessDay reports on "the year-end P&L trap," observing that investor discipline often slips during the final quarter. The publication highlights how October can effectively become a trading instruction, influencing investor behavior as the year concludes.

This phenomenon suggests that investor decisions in the final quarter, potentially starting in October, may be driven more by year-end P&L pressures than by fundamental analysis or long-term strategy. For fund managers, this could indicate periods of increased market volatility or irrational trading patterns as participants prioritize short-term P&L outcomes. Small exceptions, rather than dramatic final trades, are noted as having a significant impact.

Investors should monitor market behavior for signs of reduced discipline, particularly from October onwards. The observation that small exceptions matter more than dramatic final trades suggests attention to subtle shifts in trading rather than just headline-grabbing transactions. This implies a need for vigilance regarding year-end portfolio adjustments and potential market distortions.
