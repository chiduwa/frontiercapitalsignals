Malawi24 reports that Energy and Mining Minister Jean Mathanga defended the government's open tender system for fuel procurement. Mathanga argued that government-to-government (G-to-G) arrangements could create corruption loopholes. She noted that G-to-G deals often involve intermediaries, which increase opportunities for malpractice.

The government's stated preference for an open tender system over G-to-G arrangements indicates a focus on transparency in fuel supply. This policy aims to mitigate corruption risks in a critical sector, potentially fostering a more predictable and stable operating environment for businesses dependent on fuel, or those seeking to participate in Malawi's energy market. Reduced malpractice could also improve market integrity.

Investors should observe the effective implementation of this open tender system and its impact on fuel supply stability and pricing.
