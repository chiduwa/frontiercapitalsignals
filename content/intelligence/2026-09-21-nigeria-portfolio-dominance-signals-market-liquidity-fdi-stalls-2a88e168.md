Nigeria’s recent capital inflows reached $10.37 billion, with portfolio investments accounting for an overwhelming 95% of this total. This signifies a strong reliance on short-term, liquid capital, while foreign direct investment (FDI) into the economy remains largely stagnant.

This dynamic presents a mixed signal for investors. High portfolio fund dominance offers market liquidity and potential for agile positioning, but also introduces significant volatility. The stalled FDI, however, points to persistent structural challenges within Nigeria that could hinder long-term economic stability and growth, making real sector commitments less attractive.

Investors should closely monitor government policy shifts aimed at improving the ease of doing business and attracting genuine FDI, not just portfolio flows. Key indicators include regulatory reforms, infrastructure development, and consistent currency management. Watch for signs of sustained efforts to de-risk long-term investments.
