Nyasa Times reports that the Lilongwe City Council is advocating for tougher penalties for vandalism, stating that lenient sentences are failing to deter the theft of public infrastructure. Approximately 200 street lights have been stolen, particularly along Presidential Way, despite ongoing efforts to track down and arrest those responsible.

This situation highlights ongoing challenges with asset security and the rule of law within Malawi's capital. For international investors, especially those considering public-private partnerships or infrastructure development, such incidents introduce operational risks. These include potential for increased security costs, higher maintenance expenses, and the erosion of asset value, all of which can impact project viability and returns.

Investors should monitor the Lilongwe City Council's advocacy efforts and any judicial responses aimed at increasing penalties for vandalism. Progress in deterring infrastructure theft could signal an improving environment for long-term capital deployment and the protection of assets in Malawi.
