The Independent (Uganda) reports that the International Energy Agency (IEA) has warned that the Middle East conflict and disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz are expected to reshape global energy markets for years.

This development matters as the IEA expects countries to reconsider their dependence on imported fuels. Consequently, they will seek more secure and affordable energy supplies. For investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, this global shift could potentially create new opportunities or accelerate existing trends towards domestic energy solutions or diversified energy imports.

Investors should watch for how governments in these markets react to this IEA warning and potential changes in global energy supply dynamics. While the report does not detail specific local impacts, any move towards energy independence or new supply partnerships could influence long-term infrastructure and energy sector investments.
