Africa Briefing reports that Ghana is committing $200 million to preparations for its first nuclear power station. This move revives a long-standing ambition for the country, with construction potentially costing between $15 billion and $30 billion. The government has insisted on full state ownership for the project.

This initiative represents what could become Ghana's most expensive infrastructure project, signaling significant opportunities in related sectors. The initial $200 million commitment and the projected multi-billion dollar construction costs indicate a long-term pipeline for engineering, construction, and potentially specialized equipment suppliers. Investors should note the government's stance on full state ownership, which dictates the nature of potential partnerships or financing structures.

Investors should monitor further announcements regarding project timelines, procurement processes, and financing mechanisms for this substantial energy infrastructure development. Details on how the state intends to fund a project of this scale, given the stated full state ownership, will be critical for understanding future market participation.
