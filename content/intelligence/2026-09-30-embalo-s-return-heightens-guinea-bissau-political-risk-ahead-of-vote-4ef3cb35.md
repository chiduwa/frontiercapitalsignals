Ousted Guinea-Bissau former president Umaro Sissoco Embalo plans to return from exile and contest the December 6 presidential election. This politically charged comeback follows his removal by soldiers less than a year ago, occurring as a new constitution significantly strengthens presidential powers.

For international investors, Embalo's reentry immediately elevates Guinea-Bissau's political risk profile. His return reopens serious questions about the nation's fragile stability and the potential for a 2025 coup, reflecting its history of volatility. The expanded presidential powers could centralize authority, but also amplify the stakes and risks of future power struggles.

Investors should closely monitor the upcoming election dynamics, including any signs of military involvement or civil unrest. Pay attention to the political climate surrounding the new constitution's implementation and how its strengthened presidential powers are exercised, as these factors will be key indicators of future stability.
