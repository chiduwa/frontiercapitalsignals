Africa Briefing reports that Russia’s state nuclear corporation, Rosatom, is pursuing four major African projects. Rosatom is holding talks with Ghana, Uganda, Ethiopia, and Egypt regarding large nuclear power stations as Russia seeks a bigger stake in Africa’s energy future. Additionally, Egypt is weighing two more El Dabaa reactors.

These potential developments could reshape the energy landscape, particularly in Ghana and Uganda, which are key markets for investors. The pursuit of large nuclear power stations indicates significant long-term infrastructure investment opportunities and a strategic shift in energy generation for the involved countries. Investors should consider the scale of these projects and their potential impact on regional power stability and economic development.

Africa Briefing notes that costs and financing remain major obstacles for these initiatives. Investors should closely monitor any progress on funding arrangements and the specific financial structures of potential deals. Updates on the finalization of agreements, especially those involving Ghana and Uganda, will be crucial for identifying actionable investment signals within the energy and associated infrastructure sectors.
