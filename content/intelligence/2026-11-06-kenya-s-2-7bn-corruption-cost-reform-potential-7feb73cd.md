Africa Briefing reports that corruption and collusion add an estimated $2.7 billion annually to Kenya's fiscal deficit. It also notes World Bank analysis identifying up to $900 million in potential railway construction savings.

This highlights significant inefficiencies and risks for businesses operating in Kenya, particularly those involved in public contracts or relying on government infrastructure projects. The potential for substantial savings in infrastructure, like railways, suggests opportunities if reforms are enacted.

Investors should monitor progress on ambitious governance reforms, as Africa Briefing indicates these could lift Kenya's GDP by 22.3 percent by 2035 relative to a no-reform scenario. The implementation of such reforms could unlock significant economic value.
