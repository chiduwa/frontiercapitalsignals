Saudi-Ethiopian tycoon Mohammed al-Amoudi acquired Morocco's sole refinery, Samir, in 1997. Over a decade, the plant was systematically dismantled and allowed to fail, leaving the kingdom importing 90% of its fuel. This strategic asset, once vital for national energy security, became a symbol of a broken promise and a costly legacy.

This case offers crucial lessons for investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda. It underscores the risks of inadequate regulatory oversight when critical national assets pass into private hands, particularly with single owners. Asset stripping, mismanagement, and capital flight can severely destabilize a nation's infrastructure, impacting economic growth, supply chain reliability, and national security. Investors must assess regulatory frameworks and owner track records rigorously.

Monitor national policies and regulatory reforms aimed at protecting strategic assets. Look for increased transparency requirements, stricter enforcement mechanisms for asset integrity, and local content or operational continuity clauses in privatized infrastructure deals. Governments in the region are likely to learn from such failures, potentially leading to more robust oversight. Focus on sectors like energy, logistics, and utilities where such risks could manifest.
