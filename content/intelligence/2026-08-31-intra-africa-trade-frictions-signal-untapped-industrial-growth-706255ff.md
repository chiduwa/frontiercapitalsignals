A recent World Bank and France’s development bank report indicates Africa trades a higher proportion of processed goods internally than externally. However, this intra-continental trade volume remains insufficient to drive meaningful industrialization across the continent. The report identifies various 'trade frictions' as the primary impediment.

This dynamic presents a clear investment signal. Frictions imply inefficiencies, but also immense potential for value creation once addressed. Companies operating in logistics, trade finance, digital platforms facilitating cross-border transactions, or those focused on improving regulatory alignment stand to gain significantly. Investment in infrastructure supporting regional value chains, especially for processed goods, becomes critical.

Monitor policy initiatives aimed at reducing these identified trade frictions, particularly within the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) framework. Watch for government and development bank commitments to infrastructure improvements and harmonized trade policies. Early movers addressing specific bottlenecks will capture substantial market share as intra-Africa trade scales.
