US cuts to UN funding are accelerating the unravelling of the AU peacekeeping mission in Somalia. This withdrawal is triggering a regional scramble, with neighbouring powers actively vying for influence over an increasingly fractured state.

While Somalia remains largely uninvestable, this dynamic significantly increases regional instability. Neighboring countries, including Ethiopia and Kenya, are now deepening their engagements, creating new political and security risks that could impact cross-border trade, infrastructure projects, and maritime security in East Africa. Investors should factor in heightened geopolitical risk for assets in the broader region.

Monitor the specific actions of Ethiopia, Kenya, and other regional actors in Somalia, noting any unilateral military or economic initiatives. Watch for increased refugee flows or heightened extremist activity impacting border regions, particularly along the Kenya-Somalia frontier. Any signs of wider regional security architecture shifts or new international involvement will be critical.
