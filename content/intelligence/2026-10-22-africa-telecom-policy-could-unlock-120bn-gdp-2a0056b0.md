Africa Briefing reports a new GSMA study suggests over $120 billion in additional economic output is possible across Africa over the next decade. This potential gain is linked to governments changing how they price and license mobile spectrum.

This policy shift matters for telecom operators in markets like Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda. African operators currently pay more for less spectrum. Reforming spectrum pricing and licensing could reduce costs for operators and potentially stimulate service growth, although cheaper licenses will face a consumer test. For investors, this indicates a significant upside potential for the digital economy and related sectors.

Investors should monitor specific government policy changes regarding mobile spectrum pricing and licensing across the continent. The timeline for realizing the reported $120 billion in GDP gains depends directly on these reforms being implemented.
