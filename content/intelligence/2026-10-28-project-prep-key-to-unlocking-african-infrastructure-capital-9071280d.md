Africa Briefing reports that numerous promising infrastructure and development projects across Africa and the Caribbean continue to struggle in attracting necessary investment, despite years of discussions regarding significant opportunities. A key factor identified as holding back investment is poor project preparation, indicating that the capital gap extends beyond just the availability of funds.

For investors, this signals that the scarcity of capital is not necessarily due to a lack of interest, but rather a bottleneck in project readiness. The report suggests that well-prepared projects, especially those exploring blended finance models, are better positioned to attract private capital. This insight points to a critical area for due diligence and opportunity creation.

Moving forward, investors should closely monitor initiatives aimed at strengthening financial connections within the region, as Africa Briefing indicates these could unlock further investment opportunities. The focus will likely remain on enhancing project preparation standards and leveraging innovative financing structures to bridge the existing capital gap.
