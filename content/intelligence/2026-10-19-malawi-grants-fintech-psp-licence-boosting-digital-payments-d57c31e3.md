Nyasa Times reports that the Reserve Bank of Malawi (RBM) has granted a Payment Service Provider (PSP) licence to PayChangu, a locally owned fintech group. This licence, issued under the Payment Systems Act of 2016, allows PayChangu to offer payment aggregation services across Malawi. The report notes this development supports Malawi's drive towards a more digitised economy.

This regulatory approval for PayChangu signals a clear commitment from the RBM to foster digital payment expansion. For investors, it indicates a growing and increasingly regulated market for fintech services within Malawi. The authorization of a local player for nationwide aggregation suggests potential for increased financial inclusion and a broader shift in consumer and business payment habits, creating opportunities in supporting digital infrastructure or related financial services.

Investors should monitor the implementation and market impact of PayChangu's services once the licence becomes effective from September 30, 2026. Further developments under the Payment Systems Act of 2016, including potential for other similar licences or regulatory frameworks, will be important to observe. Assessing the broader adoption of digital payments will provide insights into the sector's growth trajectory.
