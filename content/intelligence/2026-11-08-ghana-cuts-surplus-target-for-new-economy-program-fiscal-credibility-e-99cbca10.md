The High Street Journal reports that Ghana plans to reduce its primary fiscal surplus target. This reduction aims to fund the US$10 billion New Economy programme, a move Sitsofe Mensah warns carries significant risks to the nation's fiscal credibility.

For investors, this policy shift suggests potential volatility in Ghana's fiscal outlook. Mensah highlights that the success of this programme hinges on whether its spending avoids past failures and delivers sustainable industrial growth. This presents both a risk to Ghana's economic stability and a potential opportunity for long-term industrial development, depending on execution.

Investors should closely monitor the implementation of the New Economy programme. Key indicators to watch include how effectively spending is managed to avoid past failures and whether it genuinely fosters sustainable industrial growth. The impact on Ghana's fiscal credibility will be a critical factor in assessing future investment climate.
