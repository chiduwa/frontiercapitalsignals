BusinessDay reports that the African Export-Import Bank (Afreximbank) and the African Petroleum Producers’ Organisation (APPO) have launched the Africa Energy Bank (AEB). This new institution aims to be a $5 billion bank, created amidst a broader retreat in energy funding across the continent.

This development signals a concerted effort by key African institutions to address financing gaps in the energy sector, potentially counteracting the trend of decreased external funding. For investors, the AEB could unlock new project opportunities or provide co-financing for existing energy ventures across the continent, particularly in regions facing funding challenges. Its existence suggests a strong push for domestic and regional capital deployment in energy.

Investors should monitor the AEB's initial funding rounds and its strategic focus. Key details regarding its specific investment criteria, target countries, and the types of energy projects it will prioritize are yet to be fully revealed. Understanding its operational framework will be crucial for identifying potential partnership and investment avenues.
