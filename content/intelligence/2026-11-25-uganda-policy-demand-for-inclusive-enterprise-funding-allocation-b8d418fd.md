Nile Post reports that an MP has called for 10% of GROW grants to be reserved for women with disabilities. The Gender Committee chairperson highlights concerns that women with disabilities risk exclusion from government enterprise funding. This exclusion is attributed to digital application barriers and stringent eligibility requirements, prompting the demand for a dedicated funding allocation.

This development signals a potential policy shift regarding government enterprise funding in Uganda. If successful, such an allocation could redirect capital to a specific demographic, potentially creating new market opportunities or empowering a previously underserved segment of entrepreneurs. It also underscores ongoing challenges with digital access and eligibility criteria in public funding programs.

Investors should monitor the progression of this demand within government policy discussions. A formal adoption of this allocation would directly influence the distribution of public enterprise funds, potentially opening avenues for businesses focused on inclusive digital solutions or those supporting entrepreneurship among women with disabilities. The outcome will indicate the government's responsiveness to addressing barriers in funding access.
