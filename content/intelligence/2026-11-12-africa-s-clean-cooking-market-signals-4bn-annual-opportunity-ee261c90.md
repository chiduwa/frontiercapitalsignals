MyJoyOnline Business reports that former Second Lady Hajia Samira Bawumia called for about US$4 billion in annual investment to hasten Africa’s shift to clean cooking. Speaking at the 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference (COP31), she highlighted inadequate funding as a factor worsening health risks, deforestation, and gender inequalities. MyJoyOnline Business states that the continent’s continued reliance on traditional cooking fuels like firewood undermines public health, environmental sustainability, and the economic empowerment of women and girls.

This call identifies a substantial market need and potential for investment in cleaner cooking technologies across Africa. MyJoyOnline Business reports that estimates from the International Energy Agency (IEA) suggest this US$4 billion annual investment is required by 2030. Private-sector investors are specifically urged to prioritize clean cooking, signaling a policy push to attract capital into this sector.

Investors should monitor specific policy developments and government incentives in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda that align with this continental push. The implementation of clean cooking strategies and the allocation of funding by African governments and development partners will indicate concrete opportunities for technology providers, infrastructure developers, and financiers in the clean energy space.
