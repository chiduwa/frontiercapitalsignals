A US federal jury has convicted former Goldman Sachs banker Asante Kwaku Berko on foreign bribery and money laundering charges. Berko was found guilty of conspiring to pay over $1 million in bribes related to a power plant development in Ghana. He now faces up to 30 years in prison.

This conviction significantly impacts investor perception of regulatory and governance risks in Ghana, and more broadly, in African infrastructure projects. It underscores intensified international enforcement against corruption, demanding robust due diligence and compliance frameworks from investors operating in the region. This can lead to increased transaction costs and project timelines, but also signals a move towards a cleaner operating environment long-term.

Investors should closely monitor follow-up actions from Ghanaian authorities and assess potential impacts on other large-scale infrastructure and energy projects. We anticipate increased scrutiny on anti-corruption compliance across the continent, particularly for complex deals involving government entities. This event reinforces the premium on transparency and ethical business practices.
