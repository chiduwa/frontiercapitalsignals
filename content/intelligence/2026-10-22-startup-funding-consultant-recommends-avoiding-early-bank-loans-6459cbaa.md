The High Street Journal reports that SME Consultant Dickson Assan suggests startups should avoid bank loans as an initial funding source. He notes that repayment with interest demands established cash flow, which is typically lacking in new ventures.

This perspective indicates a prevailing view that traditional bank financing presents significant challenges for nascent startups in these markets. For investors, it highlights that early-stage ventures may primarily rely on alternative capital sources such as personal savings, bootstrapping, and informal contributions from family, friends, or early supporters. This suggests that the initial growth trajectory of some startups may be slower or that they must demonstrate strong internal cash generation before seeking debt.

Investors should monitor the prevalence and effectiveness of these alternative funding methods across the region's startup ecosystem. Observing how banks adapt their offerings to support startups with limited track records, or if new financial instruments emerge, will be key to understanding future financing trends.
