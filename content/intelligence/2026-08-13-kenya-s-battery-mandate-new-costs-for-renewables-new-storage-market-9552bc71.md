Kenya Power is now mandating battery storage for all new wind and solar projects. This move aims to stabilize the grid, which struggles with intermittent renewable output, as the utility seeks cheaper overall electricity despite the added costs of ensuring renewable reliability.

This directly increases upfront capital expenditure for renewable energy developers in Kenya. Early tariff pushback suggests immediate challenges to project economics and potential delays. However, it simultaneously creates a significant new market for battery storage technology providers and integrated energy solutions. Existing project IRRs will face pressure, requiring re-evaluation.

Monitor the specifics of new power purchase agreements, especially how storage costs are factored into tariffs. Watch for policy adjustments, potential government incentives for battery storage, and how this impacts the competitive landscape for energy developers. We expect to see consolidation or strategic partnerships emerging in the renewables and storage space.
