Nigeria's National Theatre recently hosted its first creative economy summit outside Lagos, holding a two-day program in Kano. The event focused on enhancing practitioners' access to funding, financial literacy, and practical skills, signaling a strategic decentralization of support for the creative sector.

This initiative is significant for investors, indicating a deliberate push to expand economic opportunities beyond traditional hubs. Kano, a major northern city, presents a large, largely untapped market for creative goods and services. The government's focus on funding access and skill development for creative startups here signals an improving enabling environment, broadening the investable landscape in Nigeria's cultural, entertainment, and digital content industries.

Investors should watch for subsequent government or private sector initiatives building on this momentum in Kano and other secondary cities. Evidence of new creative enterprises launching or scaling up, particularly those leveraging digital platforms, will confirm the program's impact. This will highlight specific opportunities for early-stage and growth capital in a rapidly formalizing and expanding creative economy.
