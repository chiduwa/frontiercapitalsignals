Former Malawi telecoms regulator Daud Suleman is defending himself before Parliament over a Facebook post made the day Vice-President Chilima died in a plane crash. The post sparked widespread online speculation suggesting Suleman had advance knowledge of the tragedy, claims he is now publicly refuting.

This public inquiry, regardless of its outcome, underscores heightened political sensitivities and a lack of trust following Chilima's sudden death. Such events can amplify perceived political instability, drawing scrutiny on governance transparency and potentially deterring foreign direct investment by increasing perceived risk and uncertainty.

Investors should closely watch the parliamentary proceedings and how this situation is resolved. Monitor government efforts to restore public confidence and address underlying suspicions surrounding the crash. Any signs of prolonged political tension or erosion of institutional trust could signal broader implications for Malawi's investment climate.
