Malawians who previously escaped xenophobic attacks in South Africa are now returning there for work. Despite the past dangers, the Malawian government has stated it will not guarantee protection if they encounter further issues. This indicates a significant lack of employment opportunities within Malawi, driving citizens to seek livelihoods abroad even at personal risk.

This trend signals deep structural weaknesses in Malawi's domestic job market. For investors, this translates into potential challenges for local consumer growth, a diminished skilled labor pool, and overall economic stability. It highlights the urgent need for robust job creation, particularly in sectors capable of absorbing a growing workforce and retaining talent locally.

We should monitor specific government policies aimed at job creation and economic diversification beyond traditional sectors. Observe the impact on remittance flows and whether these funds are channeled into productive investments rather than just consumption. Any shifts in regional labor mobility policies or social stability in South Africa affecting migrant workers will also be critical to watch.
