Africa Briefing reports that the continent will require $125 billion in investment to expand and maintain its oil refineries by 2050. This comes as oil demand is projected to reach 9.2 million barrels daily, with existing refineries currently operating at 55 percent capacity. OPEC indicates this investment is crucial to address the widening gap between crude supply and the growing demand for refined petroleum products.

For investors in markets like Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, this signals substantial infrastructure development opportunities. The need to increase refining capacity and efficiency to meet future demand presents potential for private sector involvement in construction, upgrades, and related logistics. Current under-capacity suggests a strong rationale for new capital allocation in the energy sector.

Investors should monitor national energy policies and investment incentives aimed at attracting foreign capital into the refining sector across these African markets. Tracking specific project announcements, particularly those with government backing or multi-country participation, will be key to identifying actionable investment entry points.
