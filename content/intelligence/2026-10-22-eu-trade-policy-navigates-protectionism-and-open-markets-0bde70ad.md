The Independent (Uganda) reports that German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for the European Union (EU) to protect its industries from unfair competition. Simultaneously, Merz emphasized keeping markets open and avoiding a trade war. He highlighted the EU's trade deficit with China while acknowledging China's competitiveness.

This dialogue signals an evolving trade policy environment within the EU, balancing protectionist tendencies with a commitment to open markets. For international investors targeting Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, shifts in EU trade policy could impact demand for their exports, influence global supply chain decisions, and shape the broader economic climate.

Investors should monitor how the EU reconciles these objectives, particularly regarding market access and potential protectionist measures. Future policy decisions will define the global trade landscape, affecting various sectors and export opportunities for African markets.
