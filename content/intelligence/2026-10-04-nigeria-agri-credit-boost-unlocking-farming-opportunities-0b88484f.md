The Bank of Agriculture (BOA) has partnered with the All-Farmers Association of Nigeria (AFAN) to launch a dry season farming credit scheme. This initiative aims to enhance food security by financing agricultural production during off-peak seasons, a critical move for Nigeria's staple crop supply.

This partnership addresses a crucial financing gap in Nigeria's agricultural sector, potentially leading to increased local food production and reduced import dependency. For investors, this signals government-backed efforts to strengthen the agricultural value chain, creating upstream and downstream opportunities in processing, storage, and logistics. Improved food security also supports broader economic stability.

Investors should monitor the scheme's implementation scale, repayment rates, and its impact on specific crop yields. We will also watch for potential expansion of similar credit initiatives, involvement of other commercial banks, and the overall policy environment supporting agricultural mechanization and value addition.
