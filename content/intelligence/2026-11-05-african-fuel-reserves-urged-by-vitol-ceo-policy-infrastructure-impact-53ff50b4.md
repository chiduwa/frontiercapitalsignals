The Independent (Uganda) reports that Vitol Group Chief Executive Russell Hardy urged African countries to increase their fuel storage buffers. This recommendation was made as energy executives meeting in Abu Dhabi warned of the international petroleum market's limited capacity to absorb further shocks, driven by global supply chain disruptions.

This call highlights a potential strategic shift in energy policy across African nations, including Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda. Increased investment in fuel storage infrastructure, such as depots, pipelines, and logistics, could become a priority to enhance energy security. Investors should monitor government responses and potential tenders for such projects.

Investors should observe whether African governments, particularly in key markets, begin to formalize policies or announce initiatives to build these suggested fuel reserves. Any such moves would signal concrete opportunities in the energy infrastructure and logistics sectors.
