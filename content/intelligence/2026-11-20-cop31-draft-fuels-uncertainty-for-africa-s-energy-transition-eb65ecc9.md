MyJoyOnline Business reports a revised draft agreement at COP31 has removed references to fossil fuels, including an earlier proposal for countries to accelerate the transition away from coal, oil, and gas. The new text, released on Thursday, November 19, retains carbon capture. This development has drawn criticism over the direction of negotiations at the United Nations climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye.

For investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, this policy shift at COP31 introduces uncertainty regarding the global commitment to phasing out fossil fuels. The retention of carbon capture technologies also signals a potential pathway for continued fossil fuel utilization, which could influence investment in existing or new oil, gas, and coal projects in resource-rich nations. Conversely, it might temper the urgency for large-scale renewable energy infrastructure, depending on national policy responses.

Investors should monitor the final COP31 agreement and subsequent national policy adjustments in these African markets. Observe how governments respond to the diluted fossil fuel language, particularly regarding their energy transition plans and any new incentives for carbon capture or continued fossil fuel exploration and production.
