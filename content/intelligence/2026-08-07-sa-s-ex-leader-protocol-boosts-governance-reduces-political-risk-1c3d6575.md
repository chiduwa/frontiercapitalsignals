South Africa is implementing new protocol rules requiring former presidents and deputy presidents to disclose their intentions when seeking diplomatic support for foreign travel. This move follows controversy surrounding Jacob Zuma's unvetted interactions with the Gupta family in India, which raised concerns about the nation's international reputation.

For investors, this signals a proactive step by South Africa to enhance transparency and mitigate political influence, even post-presidency. A commitment to stronger governance and reducing potential political interference, however indirect, improves the overall investment climate. It lowers perceived country risk by demonstrating an intent towards institutional strengthening.

We will monitor the effective implementation and enforcement of these new rules. Watch for similar initiatives potentially emerging in other African nations grappling with issues of post-presidency influence or corruption. This trend toward greater accountability at high levels could influence governance scores across the continent.
