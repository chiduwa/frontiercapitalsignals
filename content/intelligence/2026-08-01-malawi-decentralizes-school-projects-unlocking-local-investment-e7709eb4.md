Malawi's government is piloting a new approach in Mzimba, allowing communities near three Community Day Secondary Schools to directly prioritize school development projects. This initiative follows strong community advocacy, initially pushing for perimeter fences due to security concerns.

This decentralization of decision-making creates new, granular investment opportunities at the local level. It indicates a shift towards community-driven procurement and demand for specific services like security infrastructure, construction, and potentially other educational facility upgrades, moving away from centralized mandates.

Monitor the success and scalability of this pilot program beyond Mzimba. Observe the types of projects communities consistently prioritize and the resulting procurement frameworks, as this model could unlock significant local public works contracts and new service demand if adopted nationwide.
