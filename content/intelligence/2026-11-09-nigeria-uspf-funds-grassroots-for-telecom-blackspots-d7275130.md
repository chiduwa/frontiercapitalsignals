BusinessDay reports that Nigeria's Universal Service Provision Fund (USPF) is initiating a program to address the country's persistent digital infrastructure gap. The USPF is specifically targeting 87 telecom blackspots and is turning to grassroots startups for solutions, offering N10 for innovation.

This initiative signals a policy-driven effort to expand digital connectivity, creating potential opportunities for investors in Nigeria's tech and infrastructure sectors. Support for local startups could foster innovative, localized solutions to infrastructure challenges, potentially opening new market segments in previously underserved areas.

Investors should monitor the specific implementation details of the USPF's funding and the types of grassroots innovation emerging. Further clarity on the scope and success of these efforts will be key to understanding long-term investment implications in Nigeria's digital economy.
