Ghana achieved a record GH¢148.3bn ($11.5bn) merchandise trade surplus in 2025. Exports significantly outpaced imports, dramatically strengthening the country’s external trade position. This performance, however, revealed an increasingly heavy reliance on gold, which accounted for 63.1 percent of total exports.

This surplus signals near-term economic stability and potential for Cedi strength. However, the extreme dependence on gold introduces substantial commodity price risk. Fluctuations in global gold prices will directly impact Ghana’s fiscal health, external balance, and sovereign risk, affecting returns for investors in Ghanaian assets and debt.

Investors should closely monitor global gold price trends and Ghana’s strategies for export diversification. Watch for government policy responses to manage this commodity exposure and any initiatives to build fiscal buffers. Assess the real trade balance, considering underlying volume changes versus price effects.
