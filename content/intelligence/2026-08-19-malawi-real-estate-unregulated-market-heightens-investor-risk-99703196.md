Malawi's real estate sector remains largely unregulated, despite the 2024 Real Estate Management Act establishing a regulatory council. Over a year later, the Real Estate Management Council, mandated to license agents and protect consumers, has not been made operational. This leaves buyers exposed to 'cowboy' operators, with the Minister admitting the industry lacks oversight.

For investors, this signifies elevated risk in direct real estate acquisitions and property-backed ventures in Malawi. The absence of licensing, professional standards, and consumer protection increases transactional opacity, due diligence costs, and potential for fraud or disputes. While some may eye undervalued entry points, the regulatory vacuum introduces significant uncertainty and operational hazards.

Monitor any concrete steps towards operationalizing the Real Estate Management Council, including budget allocations, board appointments, and public announcements on enforcement. Also watch for increased investor scrutiny or legal challenges arising from unregulated transactions. Until regulation is active, exercise extreme caution and conduct heightened due diligence on all Malawian real estate opportunities.
