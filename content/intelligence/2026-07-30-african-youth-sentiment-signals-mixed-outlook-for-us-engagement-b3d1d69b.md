African youth surveyed across 16 countries express guarded optimism regarding a potential second Trump administration. Over half anticipate gains in security, trade, and youth empowerment. However, a similar proportion fears increased US resource pressure and the dismantling of USAID. China maintains a stronger positive perception among young Africans.

This sentiment signals a complex geopolitical landscape influencing market stability and growth. Optimism for trade and youth empowerment points to potential for expanding consumer markets and skilled labor, attractive for long-term investments. Conversely, fears of US resource pressure could introduce regulatory uncertainty for extractive industries. Reduced USAID funding might shift development priorities, creating new avenues or challenges for private sector involvement.

Investors should monitor the specific policy positions from any future Trump administration, particularly regarding trade agreements, aid structures, and resource diplomacy in Africa. Observe how African governments adapt their engagement strategies and whether China deepens its influence as a preferred partner. Sector-specific impacts on mining, energy, and development-linked industries will be key indicators.
