Malawi's urban road reserves are increasingly consumed by informal car graveyards and scrap metal, particularly a 6-kilometre stretch in Lilongwe now hosting over 42 such sites. This uncontrolled expansion diminishes public space and escalates risks related to pollution and road safety.

This trend signals critical gaps in urban planning, regulatory enforcement, and waste management infrastructure. For investors, it indicates potential inefficiencies in land use and governance that could impact future real estate or infrastructure projects. Conversely, it highlights an untapped market for formal vehicle dismantling and recycling operations, suggesting a nascent opportunity in the circular economy sector.

Monitor government and municipal responses, specifically any planned clear-up operations or new policies addressing abandoned vehicles and waste management. Watch for regulatory tightening or private sector initiatives aiming to formalise the vehicle recycling industry. Such developments will signal commitment to urban improvement and new market entry points.
