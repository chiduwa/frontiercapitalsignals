The Independent (Uganda) reports that the People's Front for Freedom (PFF) has rejected a proposed Consociational Democracy Agreement put forth by Gen. Salim Saleh. The PFF stated it would not participate in political negotiations unless they address the detention of opposition leaders, alleged abductions, human rights concerns, and Uganda's political transition.

This rejection suggests ongoing political friction and highlights unresolved governance issues in Uganda. For investors, continued disputes over power-sharing, political detentions, and human rights can signal increased political risk and potential instability. This may affect long-term policy predictability and broader investor confidence, as the PFF's stance indicates significant political concessions may be needed for national unity discussions to advance.

Investors should monitor developments regarding further attempts at political negotiations and the government's response to the PFF's demands. The progress, or lack thereof, in addressing human rights concerns and advancing a political transition will be key indicators of Uganda's evolving political stability and investment climate.
