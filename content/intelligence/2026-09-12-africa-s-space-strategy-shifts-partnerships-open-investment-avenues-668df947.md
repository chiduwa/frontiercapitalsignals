Tidiane Ouattara, the newly appointed president of the African Space Agency (AfSA), has outlined a strategic vision for the continent's space sector. He advocates for extensive international partnerships, explicitly rejecting the pursuit of full technological sovereignty as impractical.

This pragmatic approach signals a welcoming environment for international investors. AfSA's emphasis on partnerships over proprietary control means lower barriers to entry and clearer pathways for collaboration in sectors like satellite technology, data analytics, earth observation, and digital infrastructure. It de-risks initial investments by leveraging existing global expertise and infrastructure.

Investors should closely monitor AfSA's upcoming initiatives, especially concrete partnership announcements with global space entities, tech companies, or other governments. Watch for policy developments from member states, including Ghana, Nigeria, and Kenya, that align with this collaborative vision to facilitate private sector participation and capitalize on emerging space-related economic opportunities.
