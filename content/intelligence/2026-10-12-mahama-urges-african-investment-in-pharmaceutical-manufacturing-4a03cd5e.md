Africa Briefing reports that Ghana's President John Dramani Mahama has called for an African-owned drug industry, highlighting that Africa currently imports over 70 percent of its medicines. He noted this dependence costs the continent billions of dollars that could otherwise support local factories and create skilled jobs.

This initiative signals a potential push towards localizing pharmaceutical production across the continent. For investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, this could mean new opportunities in drug manufacturing, supply chain development, and related services. The emphasis on common regulations could significantly expand regional market access for locally produced medicines.

Investors should monitor developments regarding specific policies or incentives aimed at attracting investment in African drug factories. Any progress toward implementing common regional regulations for pharmaceuticals would be a key indicator for expanded market potential.
