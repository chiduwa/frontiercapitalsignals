MyJoyOnline Business reports that Ghana’s economic outturn for 2026 supports claims of advancing stabilization. Real GDP grew 6.2% in the first half of 2026, and headline inflation was 5.5% in October. The commitment-basis primary surplus reached 1.4% of GDP from January to October, with the cash deficit at 2.3% of GDP against a programmed 4.5%. Public debt stood at 47.5% of GDP at the end of October.

These macroeconomic developments suggest a generally favorable direction for Ghana, providing a stronger foundation for sustained economic stability and growth. The report highlights falling average commercial-bank lending rates, now about 15.9%, alongside a substantial 34% year-on-year expansion in private-sector credit in September. This combination indicates improved credit conditions and increasing business activity, potentially signaling a more attractive environment for investment.

Investors should monitor the consistency of these positive trends, particularly the continued decline in lending rates and sustained private-sector credit growth. Sustaining this macroeconomic stability will be crucial for solidifying the foundation for future economic growth and attracting further investment.
