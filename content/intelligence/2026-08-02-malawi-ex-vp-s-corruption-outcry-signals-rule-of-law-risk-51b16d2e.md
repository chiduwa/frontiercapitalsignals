Former Vice-President Michael Usi has publicly condemned "selective justice" in Malawi. He expressed deep frustration that politicians allegedly involved in corruption and theft of public resources are not facing arrest. His strong remarks on Times Television underscore a perceived failure in the country's anti-corruption efforts.

This situation signals significant governance risks for foreign direct investment. A perceived lack of accountability for high-level corruption erodes confidence in the rule of law and institutional integrity. It suggests an uneven playing field, where political connections might outweigh legal processes, increasing operational and reputational risks for businesses.

Monitor any official government response to Usi's allegations and concrete actions taken against named or implicated officials. Observe the pace and impartiality of ongoing anti-corruption investigations. Investor sentiment and the political stability landscape, particularly ahead of any electoral cycles, will be key indicators.
