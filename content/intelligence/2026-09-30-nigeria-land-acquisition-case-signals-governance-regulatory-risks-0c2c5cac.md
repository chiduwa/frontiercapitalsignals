A lawsuit alleges the FCT Minister Wike's son received $2.1 million in cash last year to facilitate land acquisition in prime Abuja locations. He recently submitted his American passport as part of the ongoing controversial case, signaling deepening legal scrutiny into the transaction.

This development underscores persistent transparency and rule of law challenges in Nigeria's real estate sector, especially in transactions involving politically exposed persons. Such allegations increase perceived corruption risk, impacting investor confidence and the predictability of land titles, a critical factor for large-scale developments.

Monitor the outcome of this lawsuit closely, as it will signal the judiciary's independence and the government's commitment to tackling corruption involving high-profile individuals. Further scrutiny of Abuja's land allocation processes and potential reforms could follow, affecting project timelines and investment security.
