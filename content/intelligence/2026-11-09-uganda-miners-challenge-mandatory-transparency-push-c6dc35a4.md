The Independent (Uganda) reports that miners in Uganda are challenging a push for mandatory transparency laws. Kenneth Asiimwe, CEO of the Uganda Association of Artisanal and Small-scale Miners (UGAASM), advocates for practical economic incentives to encourage transparency instead of additional legal requirements. Asiimwe states that companies are unlikely to invest in audits, reporting, and disclosure if these activities are made mandatory.

This indicates potential friction between the mining sector and regulators regarding disclosure obligations. Investors in Uganda's mining sector should note the industry's preference for incentive-based compliance over stricter mandates. This stance could influence the operational costs and regulatory burden for mining companies operating in the country.

Future developments will likely involve ongoing discussions between mining associations and policymakers regarding the implementation and enforcement of transparency measures. Investors should monitor whether a compromise is reached or if new mandatory regulations are pursued, potentially altering the investment landscape for mining operations in Uganda.
