A parliamentary inquiry revealed Mzuzu Airport has operated for over ten years without functional air navigation equipment or basic radio communication recording systems. This startling admission follows the fatal crash involving former Vice-President Chilima, raising serious questions about aviation safety standards in Malawi.

This exposure highlights significant infrastructure deficiencies and regulatory oversight weaknesses within Malawi's aviation sector. For investors, it signals potential operational risks, impacts on logistics reliability, and reduced confidence in critical public services, potentially affecting tourism and business travel growth. The lack of basic safety infrastructure could deter foreign direct investment requiring robust transport links.

Investors should monitor the government’s immediate response to these findings, specifically focusing on any emergency funding allocated for airport upgrades and regulatory enforcement. Observe the outcomes of the aviation safety inquiry and any subsequent policy reforms aimed at strengthening oversight across the national transport infrastructure. Any new tender processes for equipment will also be key.
