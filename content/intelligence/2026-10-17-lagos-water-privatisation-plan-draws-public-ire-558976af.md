Premium Times reports that a coalition of CAPPA, activists, and a labour group have rejected water privatisation and metering plans in Lagos. Instead, these groups are demanding public investment in the water sector. A quoted individual noted that for profiteers, control of water is becoming like the new petroleum.

This development is significant for investors considering water infrastructure projects in Lagos. Strong public and labour group opposition to privatisation and metering could translate into regulatory challenges, project delays, or heightened political risk for private sector involvement. The sentiment comparing water control to petroleum for profit-seekers highlights the contentious nature of private ownership in this essential service.

Investors should closely monitor future policy announcements from the Lagos government regarding water infrastructure and management. Observing how this public resistance is addressed, and whether there are any shifts towards public-private partnerships or purely public investment models, will be key to understanding the viability of future water sector opportunities.
