Malawi's Agriculture Minister Roza Mbilizi publicly urged African governments to grant farmers, youth, women, and the private sector greater influence over agricultural policymaking. She argued governments can no longer be the sole dominant force in sector transformation.

This high-level advocacy signals potential policy shifts towards reduced state control and increased private sector participation in Malawi's agriculture. For investors, this could translate into a more favorable operating environment, fewer regulatory bottlenecks, and new opportunities across agricultural value chains, from production to processing and technology.

We will watch for concrete policy proposals, legislative reforms, or new public-private dialogue mechanisms in Malawi that reflect this 'let go' approach. Broader regional adoption of similar sentiments by other African governments should also be monitored as a potential continent-wide trend.
