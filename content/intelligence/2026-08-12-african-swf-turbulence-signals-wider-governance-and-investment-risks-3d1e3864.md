Sovereign wealth funds in Morocco and Gabon are struggling with governance and strategic direction. Morocco's Ithmar Capital still lacks a clear purpose a decade on, while Gabon's strategic fund navigates a post-Bongo transition.

These challenges signal broader governance issues and potential policy instability that can affect investment landscapes. While not directly in our core markets, SWFs often catalyze significant projects, and their disarray can delay key infrastructure or strategic sector investments, impacting investor confidence and deal flow across the continent.

Investors should monitor future reforms and leadership changes within these funds. Success or failure in redefining their mandates could influence governance standards and investment climates across other African nations. Look for signs of clearer strategic objectives or improved transparency, which could unlock new co-investment opportunities.
