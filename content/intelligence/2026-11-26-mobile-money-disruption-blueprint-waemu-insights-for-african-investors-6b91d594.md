TechCabal reports that Wave's Public Policy Lead detailed how the company disrupted mobile money 'cartels' in the WAEMU region by forcing down prices. The Lead also outlined ongoing structural and regulatory hurdles that limit full participation of informal businesses and women in the digital economy.

This development in WAEMU signals the potential for significant market disruption and increased competition within Africa's mobile money sector. Investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda should consider how similar low-fee strategies could reshape digital finance landscapes, potentially opening new opportunities or pressuring existing players. It also highlights the untapped growth potential if these hurdles preventing informal businesses and women's participation are addressed.

Investors should monitor the evolution of digital economy policies across the region. Specifically, watch for regulatory reforms or infrastructure initiatives aimed at overcoming the structural and regulatory barriers that currently exclude informal businesses and women. Progress in these areas could unlock substantial growth in financial inclusion and the broader digital economy.
