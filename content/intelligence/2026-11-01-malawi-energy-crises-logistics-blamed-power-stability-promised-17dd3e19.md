Nyasa Times reports that President Peter Mutharika has attributed Malawi's fuel shortage to logistics and "man-made" problems. He stated that fuel supply is not the issue, noting its availability at regional ports like Tanzania, Nacala, Beira, and Tanga. Mutharika also promised that blackouts would cease by December.

These developments directly impact business operating costs and reliability in Malawi, particularly for sectors reliant on consistent power and fuel for transport or production. While fuel is reportedly available at ports, its domestic shortage suggests significant logistical bottlenecks or internal distribution challenges, which could persist. The promised end to blackouts by December offers a potential timeline for improved power stability, though the underlying "man-made" problems hint at political or administrative hurdles.

Investors should closely monitor the progress on resolving these stated logistical and "man-made" issues, and whether the December deadline for ending blackouts is met. The discrepancy between fuel availability at regional ports and domestic shortages indicates deeper systemic challenges in supply chain management or distribution that require resolution for sustained stability.
