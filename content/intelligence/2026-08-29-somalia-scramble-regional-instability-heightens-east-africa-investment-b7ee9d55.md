The unravelling AU peacekeeping mission in Somalia, exacerbated by US UN funding cuts, has triggered a new regional power scramble. Neighbouring states are now directly vying for influence in Africa's most fragile nation, further fragmenting its political landscape.

While direct investment in Somalia remains extremely high risk, this regional power vacuum significantly elevates geopolitical instability across the Horn of Africa. Investors in neighbouring Kenya, Uganda, and even Ethiopia should monitor potential spillover effects impacting security, trade routes, and infrastructure development. Niche opportunities may arise in security services or logistics supporting regional stabilization.

Closely watch the actions of Ethiopia, Kenya, and other regional actors as they intensify their engagement. Monitor any new security partnerships or diplomatic initiatives, and assess their impact on existing regional trade agreements and infrastructure projects. The durability of state institutions in bordering countries will be key.
