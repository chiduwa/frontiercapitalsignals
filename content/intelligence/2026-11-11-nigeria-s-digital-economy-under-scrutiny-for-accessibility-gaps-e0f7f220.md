TechCabal reports that when Adedolapo Oguntayo-Williams arrived at a conference hosted by one of Nigeria’s premier banks in 2024, there was no ramp for her daughter's stroller. This incident, involving a multi-billion-naira financial institution, raised questions about the chances persons with disabilities have inside Africa’s digital economy, according to the report.

This basic infrastructure oversight points to potential systemic gaps in physical accessibility, which could mirror or impact digital accessibility across key sectors in Nigeria and potentially wider Africa. For investors, this signals overlooked market demand for inclusive design, assistive technologies, and infrastructure solutions that cater to persons with disabilities. Furthermore, future regulatory pushes for accessibility standards could create new compliance-driven investment opportunities.

Investors should monitor policy developments related to disability inclusion and accessibility mandates in Nigeria's financial and technology sectors. Observe new startups or existing firms addressing these gaps with innovative digital or physical solutions. The potential costs and timelines for improving such fundamental infrastructure across the continent also warrant attention.
