An Op-Ed highlights the deep unsustainability of Nigeria's 26 public stadiums, built for national ambition but now largely underutilized. The current model fails to generate consistent revenue, with only one stadium proving viable.

This signals a potential shift in how Nigeria views and develops its sports infrastructure. The proposed 'sport district' model suggests opportunities in real estate development, urban planning, and ancillary services, potentially via public-private partnerships. Investors should watch for reforms aimed at monetizing these significant, currently dormant assets.

Monitor any government policy statements, pilot programs, or tenders emerging from Abuja regarding new stadium development or the revitalization of existing sites. Movement towards private sector involvement in these projects would confirm a pivot from the traditional public funding model.
