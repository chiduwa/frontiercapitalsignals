James Mworia, after two decades at Centum, is now leading Kenya's National Infrastructure Fund. His mandate is to commercially deploy public capital and attract the significant private investment needed for the ambitious KSh5trn fund.

This appointment signals a serious commitment to leveraging private capital for Kenya’s infrastructure development. Mworia's track record at Centum suggests a results-oriented approach, creating potential for commercially viable investment opportunities within the fund's ambit.

Investors should monitor the fund’s initial project pipeline, its private sector engagement strategy, and the specific vehicles for co-investment. Success in early deployments will be critical in demonstrating the commercial viability of this public-private partnership model.
