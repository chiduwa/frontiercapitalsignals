Tidiane Ouattara, the first president of the African Space Agency since 2024, is pushing a clear strategy for Africa's engagement in space. He advocates for multiple international partnerships to transform space capabilities into economic power, viewing full technological sovereignty as an impractical goal.

This approach is significant for investors. It signals a pragmatic and collaborative direction for Africa's nascent space economy, creating direct opportunities for global technology firms, satellite service providers, and infrastructure investors. The emphasis on partnerships suggests a lower barrier to entry and reduced risk for international players seeking to collaborate on space-related ventures across the continent.

Investors should closely watch for specific partnership announcements from AfSA and individual African countries. Pay attention to regulatory frameworks that promote foreign investment and technology transfer in key space sectors, including telecommunications, Earth observation, and data analytics. This indicates where initial capital and expertise will be most welcomed.
