Malawi's Centre for Democracy and Economic Development Initiatives (CDEDI) is intensifying pressure on the Minister of Lands, Housing and Urban Development to publicly disclose land allocation records and the findings of a land audit. This demand comes amidst growing allegations of systemic corruption within the ministry.

For international investors, opacity in land allocation creates significant operational and reputational risks. Unclear property rights, the potential for graft, and uncertain land tenure can hinder project development, raise costs, and undermine long-term asset security in Malawi. This situation directly impacts the ease of doing business.

Investors should closely monitor the Minister's response to these demands, particularly any commitment to transparency or the full disclosure of audit results. Watch for further actions by CDEDI or other civil society groups, and any government reforms addressing land governance, as these will indicate Malawi's commitment to improving its investment climate.
