Malawi's Reserve Bank has implemented strict new rules, limiting individuals and visitors to physically possess no more than $1,000 in foreign currency without explicit central bank permission. This constitutes a dramatic crackdown on cash and foreign exchange movement.

This policy unequivocally signals severe foreign exchange scarcity and the implementation of tighter capital controls. For international investors, it elevates operational risk, complicates liquidity management, and raises significant concerns regarding profit repatriation and international transaction ease. Such measures often deter new foreign direct investment.

Investors must closely monitor the enforcement rigor of these rules and their immediate impact on Malawi's business climate and potential emergence of a parallel currency market. Key indicators to watch are any further restrictions on currency convertibility or capital flows, alongside evolving foreign investor sentiment.
