Malawi's government has abandoned its digital Farm Inputs Subsidy Programme (Fisp) for a return to traditional paper coupons. This K111bn program annually provides discounted fertiliser and seeds to over a million households. The U-turn follows previous corruption allegations within the scheme.

This policy reversal flags significant governance and transparency risks for investors. Moving away from digital systems, especially given Fisp's scandal-ridden past, could exacerbate corruption, increase operational inefficiencies, and erode investor confidence in Malawi's regulatory environment and public finance management. This directly impacts agricultural sector stability and broader economic predictability.

Investors should closely monitor the practical implementation of this paper-based system. Watch for any reported corruption cases, supply chain disruptions, or impact on agricultural output. Government accountability and swift responses to integrity issues will be critical in shaping investor perception regarding Malawi's commitment to anti-corruption measures.
