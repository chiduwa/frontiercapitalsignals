Djibouti, positioned crucially near Yemen, faces intensified pressure to remain neutral as the Houthi conflict escalates. Its economy is deeply tied to its critical ports and trade links. US and Chinese military presences nearby compound the geopolitical tension.

This directly impacts Red Sea shipping security, a vital artery for global trade, including goods bound for East Africa. Escalation or instability in Djibouti could disrupt supply chains, elevate shipping costs, and affect regional import, export dynamics for Kenya and Uganda. Geopolitical risk premiums across the East African corridor would likely rise.

Watch Djibouti's diplomatic actions and security posture closely. Key indicators include any changes in port operations, Red Sea shipping insurance rates, and the broader regional response to Houthi activities. Major power engagement will signal potential risk shifts.
