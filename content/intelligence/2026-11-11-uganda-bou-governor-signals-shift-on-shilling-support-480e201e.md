The Independent (Uganda) reports that Bank of Uganda Governor Dr Michael Atingi-Ego has cautioned Parliament’s COSASE committee that the central bank cannot sustainably defend the Ugandan shilling against depreciation. He stated that the shilling's long-term stability will depend on increasing exports, tourism earnings, and domestic production.

This statement suggests a potential shift in the Bank of Uganda's approach to currency management, indicating that investors should anticipate less direct central bank intervention to stabilize the shilling. Its stability will increasingly rely on fundamental economic drivers rather than monetary policy actions, which could influence foreign exchange risk assessments for investments in Uganda.

Investors should closely monitor Uganda's export performance, growth in tourism earnings, and domestic production figures. These economic indicators will be key to understanding the shilling's future stability, as the central bank signals a reduced role in direct currency defense.
