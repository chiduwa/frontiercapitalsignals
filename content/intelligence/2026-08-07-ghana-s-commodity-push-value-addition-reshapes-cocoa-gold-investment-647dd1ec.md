Ghana is aggressively moving to capture greater wealth from its primary exports. President Mahama announced that from the 2026-2027 season, half of all cocoa beans will be processed locally. Concurrently, the government will tighten control over small-scale gold exports, with GoldBod poised to capture more earnings, potentially loosening the grip of private traders.

This policy shift signals a significant drive for local value addition and revenue retention. For investors, it means potential disruption and new opportunities in Ghana's cocoa processing and gold refining sectors. Companies involved in raw commodity exports face increased regulatory oversight and a redefined competitive landscape. This move also aims to strengthen Ghana's foreign exchange position and macroeconomic resilience.

Investors should closely track the rollout of specific regulations and incentives for local processing infrastructure. Monitor the actual impact of tightened gold export controls on market dynamics and supply chains. Watch for government initiatives supporting domestic industrial capacity in both sectors, and any potential shifts in international trading partnerships.
