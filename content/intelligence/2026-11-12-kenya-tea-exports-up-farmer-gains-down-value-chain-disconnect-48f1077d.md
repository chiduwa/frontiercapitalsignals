The Standard reports that Kenya is exporting more tea globally than ever before, with rising volumes of the commodity being shipped to international markets. However, despite this increase in export activity, the growing volumes are reportedly not translating into better financial returns for the country's small-scale tea farmers.

This situation signals a potential disconnect within the agricultural supply chain. For investors in the agriculture sector, particularly those involved in sourcing, processing, or export, this trend highlights a challenge in value distribution. The pressure on farmer profitability could impact long-term supply stability and the sustainability of production.

Investors should monitor for any policy responses or market interventions aimed at addressing the disparity between export growth and farmer income. Understanding shifts in pricing structures and value chain dynamics will be crucial for assessing the long-term health and investment potential of Kenya's tea industry.
