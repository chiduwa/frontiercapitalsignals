The High Street Journal reports that ISSER emphasizes Ghana's need for affordable five-to-seven-year loans. This credit is deemed necessary to transform macroeconomic stability into business expansion, job creation, and economic diversification. Additionally, experts cited in the report call for improved deployment of mineral revenues towards productive investment.

For investors, this signals that despite achieving macroeconomic stability, Ghana's private sector growth may be constrained by the availability and cost of longer-term financing. The call for better use of mineral revenues suggests potential for increased government-led or government-backed productive investments if recommendations are adopted. This indicates a gap between current stability and desired business expansion.

Investors should monitor policy developments related to credit access and cost for businesses, particularly for five-to-seven-year tenures. Observing how Ghana plans to deploy its mineral revenues for productive investment will also be key. Progress in these areas could unlock significant opportunities for business growth and diversification.
