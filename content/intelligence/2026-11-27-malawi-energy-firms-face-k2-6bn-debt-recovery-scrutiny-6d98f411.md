Nyasa Times reports that Malawi's Parliament's Committee on Commissions, Statutory Corporations and State Enterprises sent Egenco officials away. The committee was unhappy with Egenco's explanation of a K2.6 billion payment to M&M Global Law Consultants. This payment was for the recovery of K14.8 billion owed by the Electricity Supply Corporation of Malawi (Escom).

This development signals increased scrutiny of state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and public funds within Malawi's energy sector. Investors in infrastructure or energy projects may face heightened regulatory oversight and demands for financial transparency. Such parliamentary interventions can highlight governance risks and affect investor confidence in the operational efficiency and accountability of key utilities.

Investors should monitor the ongoing parliamentary inquiry and its implications for Egenco and Escom. Any further revelations or policy responses regarding financial management within these entities could influence future investment decisions in Malawi's power sector. The resolution of this issue may set a precedent for corporate governance expectations for SOEs.
