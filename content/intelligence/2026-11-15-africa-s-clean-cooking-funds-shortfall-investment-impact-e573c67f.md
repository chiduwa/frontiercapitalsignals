MyJoyOnline Business reports that at COP31, it was revealed approximately US$1.46 billion of the US$2.2 billion pledged for clean cooking in Africa remains undelivered. These commitments were made at the 2024 Clean Cooking Summit in Paris. According to the International Energy Agency, only about US$740 million has been disbursed to 28 African countries supporting specific clean cooking projects, leaving roughly two-thirds of the promised funds unpaid.

This significant shortfall in funding suggests potential delays or scaling back of clean cooking projects across the continent. For investors, this indicates a slower pace of development in a critical energy sector, potentially impacting related infrastructure and technology investments that rely on these initiatives. It also highlights a gap between commitment and execution in African sustainable energy financing.

Investors should monitor future announcements from co-hosts like the COP31 Presidency, Tanzania, the IEA, and the Clean Cooking Alliance regarding efforts to accelerate the disbursement of the remaining funds. Further updates on specific project funding in the 28 affected countries will be key to understanding potential future opportunities or continued delays in this sector.
