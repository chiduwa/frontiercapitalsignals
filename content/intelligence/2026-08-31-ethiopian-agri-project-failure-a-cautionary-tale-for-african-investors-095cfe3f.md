Saudi Star Agricultural Development Plc, a Saudi-backed entity, acquired 14,020 hectares across Ethiopia's Gambella and Benishangul-Gumuz regions. This multi-billion-dollar agricultural project ultimately failed to deliver on its massive promises, leaving behind empty fields.

This case highlights significant risks in large-scale African agricultural investments. Investors must scrutinize land tenure agreements, local community engagement, and the operational capacity of host governments. Such failures underscore potential pitfalls in project execution and the gap between ambitious plans and on-the-ground realities. Robust due diligence beyond initial government assurances is critical.

Monitor how governments in target regions, including Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, respond to such high-profile failures. Observe any shifts in land lease policies or increased demands for transparent impact assessments. Look for successful agricultural models that prioritize local integration and sustainable practices, offering a contrast to projects like Saudi Star.
