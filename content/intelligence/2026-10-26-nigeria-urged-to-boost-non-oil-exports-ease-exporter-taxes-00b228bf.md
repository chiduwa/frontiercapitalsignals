BusinessDay reports an Oxford Professor urged the Nigerian Federal Government (FG) to cease taxing exporters and to double non-oil exports. This is part of a broader call to move away from an oil-rent economy, which currently shares crude revenues, in order to unlock growth and create jobs.

These recommendations, if adopted, could significantly reshape Nigeria's economic landscape. An end to exporter taxes and a focus on doubling non-oil exports would likely improve the operating environment for non-oil businesses, potentially boosting their profitability and growth prospects. This could signal a shift towards a more diversified and robust economy, offering new opportunities for investors in non-oil sectors.

Investors should monitor any policy discussions or concrete steps taken by the Nigerian FG in response to these recommendations. Specifically, watch for legislative or regulatory changes concerning exporter taxation and initiatives aimed at fostering non-oil export growth. The extent of the government's commitment to breaking the oil-rent model will indicate potential long-term investment shifts.
