The Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise (CPPE) has urged the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA) to link petrol import approvals directly to transparently verified supply shortfalls. The group warns that unchecked imports risk undermining nascent domestic refining capabilities and foreign exchange conservation efforts.

This recommendation underscores growing pressure to protect local refining investments, which could struggle against a flood of imports. For investors in Nigeria's energy sector, particularly downstream, this policy direction is critical for profitability and expansion. Furthermore, tying imports to actual gaps could conserve scarce foreign exchange, improving macro-stability and reducing currency risk for all international capital.

Investors should monitor the NMDPRA's response and any policy changes regarding petrol import licensing. Watch for signs of increased domestic refining output, as well as the impact on Nigeria's foreign exchange market stability. Government commitment to local content and industrialization will be key indicators.
