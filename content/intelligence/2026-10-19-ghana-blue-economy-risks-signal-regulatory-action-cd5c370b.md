MyJoyOnline Business reports that participants in a discussion about Ghana's blue economy highlighted several threats. These include illegal small-scale mining, known as galamsey, which discharges heavy metals and silt into rivers flowing into the sea. The use of banned chemicals, such as DDT and re-labelled toxic agrochemicals in fishing, was also pointed out. Other concerns discussed were shoreline erosion and sea-level rise, which are damaging homes, heritage sites, and landing beaches, alongside overfishing.

These issues signal growing environmental risks that could impact coastal real estate, tourism infrastructure, and the long-term viability of the fishing industry in Ghana. Contamination from mining and banned chemicals poses reputational and operational challenges for businesses reliant on clean water resources or marine ecosystems. The physical threats from erosion and sea-level rise increase the risk profile for coastal asset development and insurance costs.

Investors should monitor potential government and community responses to these challenges. Future regulatory actions aimed at curbing galamsey, enforcing chemical bans, or implementing coastal protection measures could create new compliance requirements or investment opportunities in environmental remediation and resilient infrastructure. The ongoing discussion itself suggests a potential push for stronger environmental governance.
