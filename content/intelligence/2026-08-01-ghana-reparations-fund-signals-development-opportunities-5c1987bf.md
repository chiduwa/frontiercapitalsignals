The Archbishop of Canterbury, during a visit to Ghana, reaffirmed support for a £100m fund from the Church of England, addressing its historical links to transatlantic slavery. This commitment was made at Cape Coast Castle, aligning with Ghana's proactive stance on pushing for concrete reparations.

For investors, this initiative highlights a growing trend of historical institutions committing capital towards rectifying past injustices. While not a direct commercial investment, the fund’s focus on slavery’s “lasting legacy” suggests potential for development-focused projects, infrastructure, or social impact investments within Ghana. It underscores an evolving global ESG landscape where historical justice is increasingly influencing capital allocation and national development agendas.
