Premium Times reports that 89 CSOs, including Amnesty, have urged the Nigerian federal government to publish its review of investment treaties. The CSOs warn that international arbitration stemming from these treaties could expose public funds to costly claims.

This development is significant for international investors as it signals increased scrutiny over Nigeria's existing and future investment protection frameworks. Potential costly claims from international arbitration could strain public funds, indirectly affecting the broader economic environment. Furthermore, the CSOs highlight that these treaties might constrain Nigeria's ability to enforce crucial environmental and climate regulations, a factor that could impact sectors with significant environmental footprints or those subject to evolving climate policies.

Investors should monitor the federal government's response to this demand and any subsequent release of the investment treaty review. The content of this review, if made public, could indicate potential shifts in Nigeria's approach to investment protection and regulatory enforcement, particularly regarding environmental and climate-related standards.
