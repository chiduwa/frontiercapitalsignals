The Independent (Uganda) reports that Uganda requires approximately US$28.1 billion, or Shs118.22 trillion, for climate action financing by 2030. In response, the Bank of Uganda (BoU) and the European Investment Bank (EIB) have signed a cooperation agreement. This deal aims to strengthen the financial sector's capacity to manage climate-related risks and attract investment.

This substantial financing requirement signals a significant pipeline of potential projects and a growing focus on climate-related investments within Uganda. The BoU and EIB partnership suggests an enhanced regulatory and support environment, which could de-risk investments and open new avenues for international capital. Investors should note the financial sector's strengthening ability to handle climate risks.

Investors should monitor specific initiatives arising from the BoU-EIB agreement, particularly how new frameworks or mechanisms are introduced to channel the reported US$28.1 billion into climate action projects. Details on the types of investments being prioritized and specific financing structures will be key to identifying actionable opportunities.
