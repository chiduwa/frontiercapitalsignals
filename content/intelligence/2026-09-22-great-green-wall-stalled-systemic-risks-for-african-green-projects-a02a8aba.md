Burkina Faso's Great Green Wall project, a flagship environmental restoration initiative, is severely stalled. Jihadist violence has forced the abandonment of restoration sites in the north and east. Beyond security concerns, systemic issues like fragmented funding, poor data, and donor reluctance to operate in conflict zones are pushing the 2030 targets out of reach.

This situation carries significant implications for investors in large-scale environmental or development projects across Africa. It underscores the profound risks associated with regional instability, project execution in complex environments, and the reliability of multi-donor funding mechanisms. The challenges in Burkina Faso highlight broader systemic issues affecting project viability and investor confidence for similar initiatives on the continent, impacting ESG considerations for frontier markets.

Monitor how the Great Green Wall initiative adapts its strategy and funding approach in the face of these setbacks. We will be watching for any new models of donor engagement or innovative financing solutions that could de-risk future climate adaptation or restoration projects. Observe security developments in the Sahel and their potential to either exacerbate or alleviate these systemic project vulnerabilities across West Africa and potentially beyond.
