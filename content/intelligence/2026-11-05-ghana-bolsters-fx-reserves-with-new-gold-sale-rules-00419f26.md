MyJoyOnline Business reports that performance under the PCI has been broadly satisfactory, with most quantitative targets met. Despite this, a decline in foreign exchange reserves has been observed in recent months. In response, Ghanaian authorities, specifically the Ministry of Finance and Bank of Ghana, have taken corrective actions. These measures include moving foreign exchange intermediation from the Bank of Ghana to GoldBod, and implementing a higher gold sale requirement for large-scale miners.

These actions signal Ghana's commitment to stabilize its foreign exchange reserves, which is crucial for currency stability and import capacity. The shift in foreign exchange intermediation and increased gold sale requirements represent direct policy interventions that could impact the operational landscape for commodity-focused businesses, particularly large-scale gold miners. For investors, this indicates proactive economic management, but also a potentially changing regulatory environment in key sectors.

Investors should monitor the effectiveness of these corrective actions in bolstering Ghana's foreign exchange reserves and their broader impact on economic stability. It will also be important to observe the practical implications of the higher gold sale requirement for mining operations and the overall investment climate in Ghana's commodities sector.
