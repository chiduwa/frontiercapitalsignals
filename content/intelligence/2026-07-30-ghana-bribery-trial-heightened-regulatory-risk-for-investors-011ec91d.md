Former Goldman Sachs banker Asante Berko is on trial in New York, accused of channeling over $1 million in bribes to Ghanaian officials. The alleged payments were intended to secure approvals for a multimillion-dollar power plant project. Berko denies involvement, but court documents refer to an official awaiting 'holy rain' in connection with the deal.

This trial directly impacts investor confidence by highlighting significant regulatory and corruption risks when engaging in large-scale infrastructure projects in Ghana. Such allegations can expose foreign investors to legal jeopardy, lengthy project delays, increased operational costs, and reputational damage. It underscores the potential for instability in deal certainty.

Investors should closely monitor the trial's outcome and any subsequent actions by the Ghanaian government regarding accountability or regulatory reform. The verdict could influence how international lenders and private equity perceive investment risk in Ghana's energy and infrastructure sectors. Watch for broader implications on due diligence requirements.
