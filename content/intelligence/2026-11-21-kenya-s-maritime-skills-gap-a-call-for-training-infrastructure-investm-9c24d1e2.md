The Standard reports that most Kenyan universities' and colleges' maritime curricula are predominantly theoretical. This is attributed to the absence of modern training infrastructure, specifically a dedicated training ship and advanced simulators.

This situation suggests a significant gap in the practical training capabilities within Kenya's maritime education sector. For investors, it highlights an unmet need for capital deployment into specialized educational facilities and technology. Addressing this deficit could enhance the quality of the seafarer workforce, potentially boosting Kenya's competitive position in the global maritime industry and creating opportunities in vocational training or public-private partnerships.

Investors should monitor any policy responses from the Kenyan government to this reported infrastructure gap. Potential areas to watch include incentives for private sector participation in maritime education development, tenders for new training facilities, or initiatives to modernize existing institutions. Such developments could open direct investment pathways.
