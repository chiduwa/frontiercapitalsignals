Mexican cartels are exploiting South Africa's isolated game farms to establish industrial-scale crystal meth production. The Sinaloa syndicate leverages isolated terrain, Asian chemical imports, and local corruption to cook precursors, driven by eye-watering profit margins in Australia.

This development signals a significant increase in organized crime activity and systemic governance risks across the broader Sub-Saharan African region. The conditions enabling these operations, such as isolated areas, porous borders, and corruption, are not unique to South Africa. Investors must account for potential impacts on supply chain integrity, regulatory environments, and the stability of local security forces in our focus markets.

Closely monitor regional responses to chemical precursor controls and anti-corruption initiatives. Evaluate heightened due diligence requirements for any investments in remote or border-adjacent areas, particularly those involving chemical imports. Assess potential for spillover of organized crime tactics into Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, and its implications for operational security and regulatory compliance.
