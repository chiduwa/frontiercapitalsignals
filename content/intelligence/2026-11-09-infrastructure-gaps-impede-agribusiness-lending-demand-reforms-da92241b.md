The High Street Journal reports that inadequate infrastructure, including poor irrigation and feeder roads, along with weak land documentation and delayed payments, increases the risk of agricultural loans for banks. The argument posits that these fundamental issues, rather than farmer default, are the primary reasons agriculture struggles to access credit.

For investors, this perspective highlights a systemic challenge impacting both the agricultural sector's growth potential and the stability of financial institutions involved in agribusiness. Addressing these critical infrastructure gaps could unlock significant investment opportunities in both sectors by reducing credit risk and fostering more robust economic activity.

Investors should closely monitor government policy initiatives and public or private investments aimed at improving agricultural infrastructure, such as irrigation projects, rural road networks, and land registry reforms. Progress in these foundational areas would signal a more favorable and less risky environment for agribusiness and financial services across the region.
