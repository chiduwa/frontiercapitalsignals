Africa Briefing reports that Ghana's President aims to stop the export of raw minerals by 2030. The country intends to change its decades-long practice of sending minerals overseas and importing manufactured products made from them. This policy shift prioritizes domestic processing and creating industrial jobs, rather than implementing restrictions.

This proposed policy signals a potential significant change for investors in Ghana's mining and manufacturing sectors. It suggests new opportunities for companies involved in mineral processing, value addition, and related industrial development. Conversely, it could pose challenges or require strategic adjustments for firms currently focused solely on raw mineral extraction and export.

Investors should closely monitor the government's plans to address the threat of expensive power, which Africa Briefing notes could hinder domestic processing. The success of this industrialization drive will depend on overcoming such infrastructure challenges and creating a supportive environment for manufacturing investments.
