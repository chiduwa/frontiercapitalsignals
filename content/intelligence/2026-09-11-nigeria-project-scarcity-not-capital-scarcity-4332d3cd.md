The UN Global Compact Network recently highlighted Nigeria's core capital challenge. Their assessment indicates Nigeria does not suffer from a lack of capital, but rather an insufficient pipeline of investable, "bankable" opportunities capable of attracting and efficiently absorbing available funds.

This insight is critical for investors. It means the primary bottleneck in Nigeria is not capital availability, but the identification, structuring, and de-risking of viable projects. For funds seeking deployment, this implies a competitive landscape for high-quality deals and a potential premium on project development and financial advisory expertise.

Monitor initiatives from both public and private sectors aimed at creating and refining project pipelines. Focus on sectors like infrastructure, agriculture, and energy, where large-scale projects are typically found. Also, watch for regulatory frameworks or policy shifts designed to enhance project bankability and investor confidence, particularly around legal certainty and dispute resolution.
