MyJoyOnline Business reports that petroleum workers in Ghana have threatened a nationwide strike. This industrial action would occur if Parliament passes the National Petroleum Authority (NPA) Bill, 2026, in its current form. The General Transport, Petroleum and Chemical Workers’ Union of the TUC is particularly concerned about Clause 103 of the bill.

This clause seeks to remove BOST Energies Limited’s exclusive mandate to develop, operate, and maintain inland petroleum depots, thereby opening the sector to private operators. The Union believes this proposed change could undermine Ghana’s control over critical petroleum storage infrastructure. While the Union supports private-sector participation, they advocate for it to be pursued through a partnership framework with BOST Energies. This situation introduces regulatory uncertainty and potential operational risks for current and prospective investors in Ghana's energy and logistics sectors.

Investors should monitor Parliament's progress on the NPA Bill, 2026, and any developments regarding the proposed strike by petroleum workers. The eventual framework for private sector involvement in petroleum depot management will be a key area to watch.
