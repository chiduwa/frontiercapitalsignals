The Attorney General, Frank Mbeta, has unexpectedly joined a High Court case as an interested party, challenging an injunction that blocks the formation of Malawi Parliament's 12 committees. This is notable as the AG was originally expected to support the Speaker, Sameer Suleman, who is contesting the injunction. The Speaker's lawyer criticized the AG's late entry and independent position.

This signals potential political and institutional friction at a high level. Such internal conflicts, especially those involving the Attorney General and parliamentary leadership, can create policy uncertainty and delay legislative processes. Investors value stability and predictable governance frameworks, which this dispute momentarily complicates. It suggests potential for judicial interference in legislative function, impacting the ease of doing business.

Monitor the court's decision on the injunction and the AG's involvement. The outcome will indicate the judiciary's role in resolving high-level political disputes and signal the balance of power within Malawi's governance. Watch for any broader political fallout or implications for the legislative agenda, which could affect investment-related policy reforms.
