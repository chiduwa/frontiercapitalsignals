MyJoyOnline Business reports Ghana's trade surplus significantly improved to US$8.80 billion in the first eight months of 2028, a substantial increase from US$6.69 billion in the corresponding period of 2025. This was underpinned by robust growth in overall export earnings, which outpaced the rise in imports. Gold, cocoa, and crude export earnings grew strongly during this period, but Non-Traditional Exports (NTEs) declined by 3%.

The substantial increase in Ghana's trade surplus indicates strengthening external accounts and potential currency stability, generally positive for foreign investment. Strong performance in key commodity exports signals resilience in core sectors. However, the decline in NTEs suggests a potential weakness or diversification challenge in other export-oriented industries.

Investors should monitor the continued performance of commodity prices and production volumes, as well as specific initiatives to bolster Non-Traditional Exports. The sustainability of Ghana's trade surplus will depend on sustained export strength relative to import demand.
