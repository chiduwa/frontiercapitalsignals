Africa Briefing reports that ECOWAS and the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) have agreed to establish joint working groups to negotiate future relations. This move represents a fresh attempt to manage the rift created when Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger left ECOWAS. No cooperation treaty was signed in Dakar.

For investors, these negotiations are significant as trade, movement, and security are reportedly at stake. A stable and predictable regional environment is crucial for business continuity and market access. The ongoing dialogue suggests a concerted effort to address tensions, which could eventually lead to improved cross-border relations and reduced operational uncertainties.

Investors should closely monitor the progress of these joint working groups. Their negotiations will define the future framework for interactions between the blocs, potentially impacting regional trade policies, logistics, and overall security dynamics. The current absence of a signed treaty indicates that a definitive resolution is still pending.
