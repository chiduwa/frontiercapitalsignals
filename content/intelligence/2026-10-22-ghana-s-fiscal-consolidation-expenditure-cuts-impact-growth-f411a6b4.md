MyJoyOnline Business reports that Ghana's fiscal consolidation in 2025 will primarily be driven by expenditure restraint, rather than growth in revenue. According to the economic research firm of the University of Ghana, this adjustment specifically involves sharp reductions in capital spending.

Smaller deficits and lower debt are expected to reduce pressure on government financing, creating conditions for greater macroeconomic stability. However, investors should note that this approach, particularly the cut in capital spending, may constrain infrastructure development. This could also impact the nation's future economic growth prospects.

We should observe how Ghana implements these expenditure restraints and the specific areas of capital spending that are affected. The long-term implications for infrastructure projects and overall economic expansion bear close watching, as they could present both risks and opportunities depending on the sectors involved.
