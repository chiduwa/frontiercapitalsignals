Burkina Faso has launched Raffinor, its first national gold refinery, capable of processing 164 tonnes annually. The government frames this as a crucial move towards economic independence and greater control over its mineral wealth.

For international investors, this development signals a growing trend of resource nationalism and beneficiation across Africa. It mandates local value addition, which could reshape supply chains, create new domestic market opportunities, and potentially disrupt established export channels for unrefined gold. Companies operating in the region must anticipate stricter local content requirements and potential shifts in export policies, as governments aim to capture more value from their natural resources.

Next, monitor Raffinor's operational efficiency and how vigorously Burkina Faso enforces domestic refining mandates on mining companies. Watch for similar policy shifts in other gold-rich nations, including Ghana and Mali. Such moves could redefine regional gold trading hubs and necessitate new strategic partnerships for investors seeking to maintain market access and comply with evolving local regulations.
