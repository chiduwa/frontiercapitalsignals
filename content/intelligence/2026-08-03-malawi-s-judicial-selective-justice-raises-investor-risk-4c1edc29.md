Former Vice-President Michael Usi recently delivered a scathing critique of Malawi's judicial system. He exposed a deeply entrenched system of selective justice, where politically connected individuals enjoy impunity, leading to systematic public resource drainage and undermining official anti-corruption efforts.

This revelation is a significant red flag for investors. A bifurcated legal system, where the rule of law is inconsistently applied, directly increases operational and political risk. It threatens contract enforceability, property rights, and overall business predictability, making capital deployment riskier and potentially deterring foreign direct investment into Malawi.

Investors should closely monitor government and judicial responses to Usi's allegations. Any meaningful commitment to judicial reform or increased accountability will be crucial. Conversely, a lack of action, or further entrenchment of selective enforcement, will signal continued systemic risk, impacting long-term investor confidence and capital allocation decisions.
