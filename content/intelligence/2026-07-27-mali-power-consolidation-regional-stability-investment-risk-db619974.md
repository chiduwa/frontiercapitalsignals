Malian junta leader Assimi Goïta has taken back the critical defence portfolio following Sadio Camara's death. He is tightening his inner circle of officers, relying on them to combat jihadists and solidify the army as the primary pillar of his regime.

This move signals a deeper entrenchment of military rule, extending political uncertainty and increasing governance risk in Mali. While Mali is not a primary focus market, its instability significantly impacts regional security and investor confidence, particularly for neighboring West African nations. This prolonged military dominance suggests a challenging operational environment for any cross-border investments or supply chains.

Monitor the junta's effectiveness against persistent jihadist threats and any potential spillover effects into neighboring countries. Observe the stability of Goïta's internal military support and any signs of popular discontent. Any changes in international sanctions or regional diplomatic pressure will also be key indicators.
