Trevor Manuel, South Africa’s influential former finance minister, openly stated that the country's democracy requires "a lot of repair work." Speaking to The Africa Report, Manuel, a respected voice, highlighted significant governance flaws within the continent’s largest economy.

Manuel’s candid assessment, coming from such a senior figure, signals a critical recognition of systemic weaknesses in a key African economy. While specific to South Africa, these comments underscore broader governance concerns that impact investor confidence across the continent. Political stability and robust democratic institutions are foundational to sustainable economic growth and reduced investment risk.

Investors should monitor subsequent political discourse and any concrete policy initiatives aimed at strengthening governance in South Africa. We also recommend observing if similar high-level acknowledgements of democratic deficits emerge in other key African markets, as such discussions often precede periods of reform or political volatility.
