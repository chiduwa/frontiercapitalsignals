Africa Briefing reports that ambitious economic cooperation plans between Africa and the Caribbean continue to face significant challenges. Costly shipping, fragmented regulations, and limited trade finance are identified as key impediments. While Afreximbank has increased financing for CARICOM to $5 billion, Kelly emphasizes the need for concrete contracts over conferences to drive progress.

For investors in Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, Malawi, and Uganda, these persistent hurdles indicate difficulty in leveraging potential new trans-Atlantic trade routes and market access. Despite Afreximbank's boosted financing, which addresses a capital gap, the fundamental operational issues of logistics and regulatory disharmony remain, limiting immediate, profitable business expansion in this corridor. This suggests a slower-than-anticipated realization of economic cooperation benefits.

Investors should closely monitor how Kelly's call for contracts translates into tangible agreements and if the Afreximbank financing leads to practical solutions for shipping inefficiencies and regulatory fragmentation. The extent to which these core, long-standing challenges are overcome will signal the true viability and timeline for unlocking trans-Atlantic trade and investment opportunities.
