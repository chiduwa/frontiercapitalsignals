Malawi24 reports that the Malawi Environment Protection Authority (MEPA) has banned the sale and storage of poultry and livestock manure along public roads and other unauthorized locations. This regulation, which includes penalties for offenders, is set to take effect on November 7, 2026. MEPA states that roadside manure trading has become widespread, raising concerns.

This policy development signals a formalization effort within Malawi's agricultural input market, specifically impacting the informal organic fertilizer supply chain. Investors in the Malawian agriculture sector, particularly those involved in farming inputs, logistics, or large-scale farming, should note potential shifts in operational costs or supply chain structures once the ban is implemented. The long lead time until November 2026 allows for market adaptation.

Investors should monitor MEPA's detailed implementation plans and how current informal manure distribution networks evolve. Observe the emergence of formal organic fertilizer suppliers or new, compliant distribution channels. Assessing potential impacts on farming costs and the availability of crucial soil inputs will be key.
