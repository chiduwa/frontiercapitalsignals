The Gambia is experiencing widespread power blackouts, triggering public protests in Banjul. This crisis stems from a significant reduction in electricity supply from Senegal and Guinea. The situation is mounting political pressure on President Adama Barrow, just months ahead of the critical December election.

For investors, this crisis signals elevated political instability and significant infrastructure vulnerabilities, particularly in critical sectors like energy. Reduced power directly impacts business operations and investor confidence. It also underscores the inherent risks of relying heavily on cross-border energy supply, a common challenge across West Africa.

Monitor the Barrow administration's immediate response to the protests and its strategy to secure alternative or increased power supply. The December election outcome will be critical, as will any broader regional discussions on energy security and diversification efforts. Further instability could impact sovereign risk perception for the wider region.
