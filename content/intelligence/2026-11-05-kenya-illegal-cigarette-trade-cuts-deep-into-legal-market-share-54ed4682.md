Capital FM Business reports that a BAT Kenya study indicates annual losses from illegal cigarette trade in the country amount to Sh10.2bn. The share of legally taxed cigarettes in Kenya is projected to decline by 11 percentage points, from 66 percent in 2021 to 55 percent in 2025.

This development signals a challenging operating environment for legitimate tobacco companies in Kenya. The shrinking market for legally taxed products directly impacts the revenue and profitability of established players. It also highlights potential challenges for government tax collection from the sector, affecting fiscal stability and potentially leading to less predictable regulatory environments.

Investors should monitor government efforts to address the illegal trade and reclaim market share for legally taxed products. Any policy changes or increased enforcement actions will be crucial indicators for the sector's future performance and the potential for recovery in legitimate sales.
